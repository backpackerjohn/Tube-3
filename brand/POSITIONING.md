## (a) The Channel Promise — one sentence

> **Every video shows you exactly how a disaster happened — the failure mechanism, the decision chain, and the system that allowed it — reconstructed from the official investigation report and drawn so you can see it.**

This is the contract every video keeps, without exception. Operationally it decomposes into four checkable clauses:

| Clause | Test before publish |
|---|---|
| "exactly how" | The video answers the mechanism question, not just the narrative question. A viewer can re-explain the failure to someone else afterward. |
| "from the official investigation report" | Every causal claim in the script cites a section of the named report (NTSB, CSB, TSB, NIST, Royal Commission, etc.). If the report doesn't say it, we don't say it. |
| "the decision chain and the system" | At least one segment covers the organizational/regulatory layer, not just the physics — the TSB's 18 interlocking causes at Lac-Mégantic is the template (slate, report-vault proposal). |
| "drawn so you can see it" | At least one custom failure diagram per video is original to us — the visual signature (memo v2 thesis). |

## (b) Audience Definition — who watches, and what job they hire us for

### The market evidence for who this audience is

The census and slate research show three distinct proven audiences already paying attention to this exact subject matter, on channels that partially — but only partially — serve them:

1. **The true-story narrative audience** is huge and loyal: Fascinating Horror (FH) holds 1.44M subs with *no main upload below ~170k views* in its last 100, on a calm, non-sensational, faceless format (census, reference row; S043). Tragedy Tales reached 482k subs on 93 videos with the same calm-narration register (census).
2. **The engineering-education audience** is even bigger and richer: Practical Engineering has 4.79M subs (S214); the USCSB — a *government agency* — averages ~715k views per video (420k subs, 73.6M views, 103 videos; S214, slate report-vault), and its BP Texas City animations alone hold 4.1M combined views. The slate notes CSB material is used in university courses — this audience treats disaster forensics as education, and it draws the engineering-channel sponsor set (Brilliant/CuriosityStream/Nebula class; memo v2 §2).
3. **These two audiences demonstrably overlap on the same topics.** The Sunshine Skyway collapse was profitably covered by FH (4.59M views), Brick Immortar (2.96M), *and* Plainly Difficult (1.45M); I-35W by all three again (996k / 1.34M / 667k) (slate, ep. 1 evidence; S201). Marquee engineering-failure topics absorb 2–3 quality treatments because the narrative viewer and the engineering viewer both show up — and no single incumbent serves both registers in one video. That seam is where this channel lives.

### Persona 1 — "The Mechanism Seeker" (core; the engineering-education viewer)

- **Who:** Engineers, technicians, safety professionals, STEM students, and lay people who think like them. The audience that made a federal agency's animation channel average 715k views/video (S214) and put 5.32M views on Practical Engineering's Oroville video (slate, ep. 7 evidence).
- **What they do today:** Watch Practical Engineering and USCSB; sit through FH videos muttering "but *why* did the eyebar fail?" — because FH deliberately keeps the mechanism layer light (memo v2 §2).
- **The job they hire us for:** *"Explain the actual failure to me — the physics, the instrumentation, the design flaw — well enough that I could explain it at work tomorrow."* They reward diagram craft and report fidelity, and they punish hand-waving in the comments.
- **Evidence they're underserved:** BP Texas City — 4.1M views on the CSB's own dated animations while the best *independent* treatment ever made has 3,272 views (slate, ep. 5; S202-class verification). Demand of that size sitting on a 2008 agency video is this persona begging for supply.

### Persona 2 — "The True-Story Completionist" (volume; the FH/Tragedy Tales viewer)

- **Who:** The documentary/true-story audience — watches disaster content the way others watch true crime, but explicitly prefers the respectful, non-graphic register (FH's entire 7-year, 1.44M-sub position is built on it; census).
- **What they do today:** Watch every FH upload; watch DisastersUncovered, whose uploads out-view its 26.6k sub count on marquee-adjacent topics (Aberfan: 478k views; census); choose "No AI. Human Narrated." titles on purpose — that label is DisastersUncovered's survival branding, i.e., this persona actively selects for human authenticity (census, evidence summary).
- **The job they hire us for:** *"Tell me the whole true story of this event — calmly, completely, and without exploiting the dead — and leave me knowing something I didn't."* The forensic layer is the "something I didn't know": it upgrades their FH habit rather than competing with it.
- **Evidence they're underserved:** FH's MGM Grand video is one of its biggest ever (1.94M views) *and* deliberately mechanism-light — and nothing of quality has followed in 5 years (slate, ep. 3). The story audience already proved demand; the explanation was never delivered.

### Persona 3 — "The Domain Professional" (small, high-value; the Brick Immortar viewer)

- **Who:** Mariners, plant operators, rail workers, structural engineers — people from the industries in the videos. Professional mariners organically recommend marine-accident YouTube channels to each other on the gCaptain forum (S118); Brick Immortar built 395k subs / 55.9M views on just 61 maritime deep-dives (S214).
- **The job they hire us for:** *"Get the technical details right, use the primary record, and take my industry's failures seriously — so I can share this with colleagues without embarrassment."*
- **Why they matter beyond their size:** They are the credibility engine — the commenters whose "this is accurate, I work in this field" replies convert Persona 1 and 2 viewers into subscribers, and the natural first members/patrons (FH's Patreon is linked in 100/100 descriptions — the membership leg works in this niche; census Q3). Brick Immortar's 2026 pivot away from disaster deep-dives (recent uploads 2.9k–17k views on Hormuz-tracking content; slate cohort-proposals) leaves this persona's supply actively shrinking.

## (d) Differentiation Mechanics — and why each is policy-defensible

Each mechanic below is both a competitive wedge and a specific defense under YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" YPP policy and the March 2024 synthetic-media disclosure rules (policy probe; S069, S071, S072).

### 1. Report-first sourcing (the originality moat)
- **Mechanic:** Every video is built from a named official investigation (NTSB HAR-71-1, CSB 2005-04-I-TX, TSB R13D0054, NIST NCST, Royal Commissions…). This is content competitors literally cannot copy without doing the same work — the memo calls the research/narrative moat "hard to copy" (memo v2 re-adjudication table).
- **Policy defense:** The inauthentic-content policy targets "mass-produced," "repetitive," reused content without original value; YouTube's own reused-content FAQ requires "significant original commentary/modification/value viewers can perceive" (S096; S069 — Rene Ritchie clarification that original AI-assisted content stays monetizable). A 15–25-source primary-document reconstruction is the *maximum-distance* profile from the enforced shape. The Jan 2026 purge hit template channels (Kapwing data via policy probe, Case 3); channels with visible research depth were not the named pattern (Grokipedia demonetization-pattern summary, S093: "automated slideshows, compilations without commentary, AI narrations lacking human-curated insight").

### 2. Custom failure diagrams (the visual signature)
- **Mechanic:** Self-made cross-sections, causal maps, and mechanism animations — eyebar fracture progression, turbine stud fatigue, smoke-path diagrams. The census attributes breakout separation in this cohort to packaging/visual craft (memo v2 §4); diagrams are also the one asset that makes the "forensic layer" position visible in a thumbnail.
- **Policy defense:** Triple-clean. (i) Self-made assets carry zero reuse/copyright exposure — versus the archival-clip collage format's constant risk (hard gate 3, memo v2). (ii) Stylized, non-photorealistic imagery is *explicitly exempt* from the synthetic-media disclosure label — the policy probe's format-by-format ruling #4: "clearly unrealistic, animated" content "fails the 'could be mistaken for real' test by design" (probe Task 4; S072, S073). (iii) Diagrams are expensive to fake convincingly, so they are a structural barrier against the AI-slop cloners who flooded this lane (census: the AI "boring history" flood; French Whisperer's 60% view collapse to slop competition, probe Case 1).

### 3. Decision-chain storytelling (the narrative engine)
- **Mechanic:** The story spine is the chain of human and institutional decisions — the hand-brake test done with air brakes set, the level indicator nobody fixed, the inspection that never happened. This is what turns Persona 1's mechanism into Persona 2's story, and it's the layer no incumbent delivers (FH keeps it light; USCSB stops at the physics; Dark Records replaces it with shock).
- **Policy defense:** Decision-chain scripts are inherently non-templatable — every event's causal web is unique (Lac-Mégantic alone has 18 interlocking causes, TSB R13D0054; slate). The enforcement wave's named target is "easily replicable at scale" template content (policy probe, Task 1 §4 — the Fall of Nations risk profile we are the opposite of). Human editorial judgment per episode is precisely the "authenticity signal" the policy climate rewards (probe Task 2: the accountable-faceless case study).

### 4. Calm register + human narration (the trust layer)
- **Mechanic:** Owner-recorded human narration, measured tone, no jump-scares, no dramatic music stabs. This is what the niche's proven audience selects for: FH built 1.44M subs on it, and the lane's one thriving young indie stamps "No AI. Human Narrated." into *every title* as a market differentiator (census).
- **Policy defense:** Two-sided. (i) Ad suitability: tragedy/"sensitive events" content is limited-ads territory by default (S071-adjacent guidelines, support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278, cited in census sources); the non-graphic, respectful house style is the documented 7-year mitigation that keeps FH-class channels monetized (census Q3; memo v2 re-adjudication: "historical non-graphic treatment is explicitly monetizable"). (ii) YPP risk: pure synthetic narration is a named monetization-risk factor (probe Task 4 ruling #1; memo hard gate 1). Human narration is not a stylistic preference — it is the binding gate that converts the field-wide faceless-channel headwind (French Whisperer, Doctor NOS; probe Cases 1–2) from an existential threat into a survivable discount.

## (e) The Audience Promise — in the viewer's words

> **"In fifteen minutes I'll understand exactly why it happened — not just what happened. They read the 300-page report so I didn't have to, they drew it so I could actually see it, and they never made me feel like a ghoul for watching."**

Per persona, the same promise sounds like:

- **Mechanism Seeker:** "I can finally see the failure — the diagram showed me what the eyebar crack actually did."
- **True-Story Completionist:** "It's like Fascinating Horror, but it answers the question FH always leaves hanging."
- **Domain Professional:** "They read the actual TSB report. I sent it to my whole crew."

The negative promise is equally binding, because the census proves this audience punishes its violation (DisastersUncovered's "No AI" titling exists because viewers demanded it): **"They will never bait me, never show me bodies, never guess, and never feed me slop."**
