TASK 4 — MARCH 18, 2024 SYNTHETIC-MEDIA DISCLOSURE LABEL: WHICH FORMATS NEED IT
=====================================================================

Policy basis: YouTube's "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content" rules (blog.youtube announcement Mar 18, 2024; help page support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 — official page fetch was blocked in this environment; language reconstructed from the official blog, TechCrunch, and multiple secondary explainers quoting it). The test: disclosure is required for REALISTIC content — "content a viewer could easily mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event" — made with altered or synthetic media. Explicit examples requiring disclosure: making a real person appear to say/do something they didn't; altering footage of real events/places (e.g., making a real building appear to catch fire); "generat[ing] a realistic-looking scene that didn't actually occur" (e.g., a realistic fictional tornado approaching a real town); synthetically generating a real person's voice to narrate. Explicitly EXEMPT: content that is "clearly unrealistic, animated, includes special effects," beauty/color/lighting filters, and "production assistance" (AI-generated scripts, outlines, titles, thumbnails, infographics). Label placement: "Altered or synthetic content" notice in the expanded description; a more prominent on-player label for sensitive topics (news, elections, finance, health); YouTube reserves the right to apply the label itself when non-disclosure could mislead.

Format-by-format ruling:

1. AI narration over charts/maps — NO LABEL REQUIRED. Charts and maps are not realistic scenes anyone could mistake for reality. A generic synthetic/TTS voice narrating is not, by itself, listed as requiring disclosure; the voice trigger is cloning "the voice of a REAL person." (Separate issue: pure TTS narration is a monetization-risk factor under the July 2025 inauthentic-content YPP policy — different policy, different penalty.)

2. AI narration over archival photos — NO LABEL REQUIRED, with two tripwires: (a) if the archival photos are themselves altered/animated in a way that changes what the real event or place appears to be, that's "alters footage of a real event or place" → label required; (b) if the narration clones a real identifiable person's voice (e.g., making Churchill "narrate") → label required. Static authentic archival imagery + generic AI voiceover = exempt.

3. AI-generated REALISTIC historical scene imagery — YES, LABEL REQUIRED. This is the policy's core example almost verbatim: it "generates a realistic-looking scene that didn't actually occur." Photorealistic AI renderings of battles, street scenes, historical figures, or period events that a viewer could mistake for photographs/footage must be disclosed via the Creator Studio "altered content" toggle. Extra exposure: history content touching wars, politics, or news-adjacent topics can qualify as "sensitive," escalating the label from the description to the front of the player, and YouTube can add the label unilaterally and penalize repeat non-disclosers (removal, YPP suspension).

4. AI-generated STYLIZED/ILLUSTRATED imagery — NO LABEL REQUIRED. Falls squarely under the "clearly unrealistic, animated" exemption — painterly, cartoon, sketch, or obviously illustrated aesthetics fail the "could be mistaken for real" test by design. This is the compliance-optimal art direction for AI-visual documentary channels: it avoids the disclosure label entirely while remaining AI-generated. (Dr. Jonathan Tam's "AI visuals where no footage exists" positioning sits exactly on the line between formats 3 and 4 — which visual register he uses determines whether each video legally needs the toggle.)
