### Verdict explanation

The attainability pillar (#1) overstates the census: 'multiple thriving young indies' is actually one thriving indie plus one 'modest' grinder, with an explicit survivorship-bias warning, and the 'every vintage mints winners / unmatched continuity' claim is contradicted by the memo's own kill table, where C10's lane shows larger and more recent cold-start winners (including a January 2026 breakout — something C09's lane entirely lacks). Finally, the OPEN/CLOSING override that flipped the tournament result was applied asymmetrically: C10 was killed for weak Mar-Jun 2026 entrants while C09 was passed with zero 2026-entry evidence and an admitted inability to observe 2026 failures. What survives the audit is a much weaker, honest case: one genuinely thriving 18-month-old indie (verified again today: 5 of its last 20 uploads clear 100k views), a 'modest' second entrant with sponsors at 7k subs, real but authority-gated demand, and elevated-but-shared policy risk. That residual case might still make C09 the least-bad finalist relative to the falsified alternatives, but the selection memo as written does not accurately represent its own evidence, and per the audit standard (default to refuted when the load-bearing claims fail verification), the selection should be sent back for re-argument on corrected facts — in particular, re-adjudicating C09 vs C10 and C23 without the fabricated-in-effect Tenerife datapoint, the 'multiple thriving indies' overstatement, and the miscast keyword contrast.

The right decision is either C23-with-mitigation or a cheap two-lane test with a payback-calibrated kill-switch — not committing to C09 on this memo.
