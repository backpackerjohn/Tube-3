## 2. Description (172 words · line 1 = human-narrated statement, per brand §4.2)

```
Researched, written, and narrated by a human.

The Silver Bridge collapse, December 15, 1967: a 39-year-old suspension bridge
carrying U.S. 35 over the Ohio River fell at rush hour, and 46 people died.
The cause was a crack roughly a tenth of an inch deep, hidden inside the eye
of one steel eyebar — a surface no inspector could see without taking the
bridge apart. Reconstructed from NTSB Highway Accident Report HAR-71-1, this
episode covers the two-eyebar chain design with no redundancy, stress
corrosion cracking and corrosion fatigue, why the flaw was physically
undetectable in 1967, and how one bridge failure created the National Bridge
Inspection Standards that still govern every U.S. bridge. Point Pleasant's
Mothman legend gets one line; the report gets the rest.

SOURCES
Official report (NTSB HAR-71-1): https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR7101.pdf
NTSB retrospective: https://safetycompass.wordpress.com/2017/12/15/the-silver-bridge-collapse-dont-blame-the-mothman/
FHWA, NBIS history: https://highways.dot.gov/highway-history/general-highway-history/happy-50th-anniversary-national-bridge-inspection-standards
Full source list in the pinned comment.
```
